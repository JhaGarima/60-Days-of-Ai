## SECTION 12 — Interview Talking Points

### Most impressive achievements to lead with
1. **Built and run a psychoeducation initiative solo** — full ownership of research, writing, editing, and publishing, with accuracy and ethics as standards.
2. **60 consecutive days of documented daily work** — publicly verifiable on GitHub/LinkedIn; concrete proof of the consistency clinical documentation demands.
3. **Honours-with-Research track plus two minors** (Computer Applications, Management) — sustained alongside independent work, showing load management.

### Key stories to prepare (use Situation → Task → Action → Result)
- **The accuracy story:** How you ensure a psychoeducation piece is correct and responsible — choosing a topic, reviewing literature, simplifying without distorting, editing, publishing. *Demonstrates: research rigour + ethical care, the JD's core.*
- **The discipline story:** Committing to the 60-day challenge — how you sustain a daily habit, what your documentation system looks like, what you do on hard days. *Demonstrates: reliability for case records and supervision reports.*
- **The translation story:** One specific time you turned a complex human-behaviour concept into content a layperson understood. *Demonstrates: the communication skill psychoeducation workshops require.*

### Questions recruiters are likely to ask — and your angle
- **"Why clinical psychology?"** Connect your awareness work to its limit: content educates at scale, but supervised clinical work is where assessment and care happen — this internship is the bridge.
- **"How do you handle confidentiality?"** State principles: client information stays within supervision channels, records are kept secure and minimal, and no clinical detail ever appears in personal or public content. Reference that ethics/confidentiality is an explicit commitment on your resume.
- **"You list AI tools — how would you use them in a clinical setting?"** Be ready for this. Safe answer: only for non-clinical tasks (formatting, literature search support, drafting general psychoeducation material), never with client data or identifying information, and always within the organisation's policy and supervisor approval. This turns a potential concern into a maturity signal.
- **"You're early in your degree — why invest in you?"** Reframe: a long runway means they can shape you, you can return across multiple semesters, and your initiative shows they won't need to push you.
- **"Tell me about managing a complex task independently."** Use the content-workflow story.
- **"What's your availability?"** Know your semester schedule cold before the interview; offer specific weekly hours.

### Smart questions for you to ask them
- "What does supervision look like for interns here — frequency and format?"
- "Which documentation systems and report formats do interns use, so I can prepare?"
- "What does a successful intern look like to you at the end of this programme?"
